Real estate investing

My First Rental Property

I bought my first investment property at 20. Beyond the financial results, it taught me that ownership means handling the unexpected and taking responsibility for the people and the place.

20Age at purchase
~$1KMonthly profit
$40KSale profit
What I learned +

The numbers on paper never tell the whole story. Toilets clog, appliances fail, sinks leak, walls need repairs, and unexpected expenses are part of the job. I've learned that staying calm, assessing the problem, and finding a practical solution matters more than getting frustrated.

I've also learned that tenant relationships require balance. I may own the property, but it's someone else's home. I want tenants to feel comfortable and respected while also making sure the property is maintained and expectations are clear. Navigating that balance taught me a lot about communication, human nature, and long-term relationships.

Entrepreneurship / college years

Pressure Washing & Industrial Cleaning

I ran a pressure washing and cleaning business while going through college to help cover my expenses, taking on everything from residential jobs to dirty and demanding agricultural equipment cleanouts.

What I learned +

Running my own business taught me a straightforward principle: do what you say you will do, do it well, and do it safely. If I promised an 8 a.m. start, that meant preparing the trailer and equipment the night before and getting supplies ready early enough to be there on time.

I also learned some safety lessons the hard way. Taking risks at heights and rushing were mistakes that changed how I approach preparation and job-site safety. And I learned when to get help: trying to handle enormous houses alone wasn't always efficient or smart. Hiring assistance, sometimes from family, improved the quality of the work and my ability to focus on the task.

The biggest takeaways were accountability, pride in a job well done, and knowing when a team produces better results than working alone.
